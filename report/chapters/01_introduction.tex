\section{Background \& Motivation}
In the contemporary era of exponential scientific output, academic researchers in Computer Science face an overwhelming volume of newly published literature each year. Thousands of cutting-edge papers are presented annually at prestigious venues such as the \textit{International Conference on Logic Programming (ICLP)}, the \textit{International Conference on Principles of Knowledge Representation and Reasoning (KR)}, \textit{Computer Aided Verification (CAV)}, and \textit{Logic in Computer Science (LICS)}.

However, an individual researcher possesses strictly bounded reading bandwidth and cognitive capacity. It is humanly impossible to digest every incoming publication. Consequently, an intelligent literature filtering and prioritization mechanism is essential:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Filtering Out Irrelevant Literature:} Papers lying outside the researcher's specialized interests (labeled as 1) must be filtered out immediately, as recommending them wastes valuable research time.
  \item \textbf{Prioritizing High-Impact Contributions:} Worthwhile publications (ratings 2 through 5), particularly breakthrough papers directly extending the researcher's ongoing inquiries (ratings 4 and 5), must be surfaced at the very top of the reading list.
\end{itemize}

The \textbf{Personalized Research Paper Recommendation (Kaggle Assignment 261)} challenge addresses this exact problem. Given historical reading preferences on 2,494 training publications scored on an integer scale from 1 to 5, participating teams are tasked with predicting and assigning continuous priority scores to 596 unrated candidate papers in the test collection.

\section{Project Objectives}
The primary objectives pursued by team \textbf{hí ộ} encompass:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Data Profiling \& Exploratory Analysis:} Thoroughly characterizing the researcher's reading preferences through empirical examination of title semantics, author networks, publication venues, and chronological trends.
  \item \textbf{Substantive Methodological Integration:} Rigorously combining foundational Data Mining paradigms taught in the course, including \textit{K-Means Clustering}, \textit{Association Rules Mining}, and Gradient Boosted Decision Trees (\textit{GBDT}).
  \item \textbf{Two-Stage Recommender Architecture:}
    \begin{itemize}
      \item \textit{Stage 1 (Precision Filter):} A high-precision binary classification model designed to purge rating-1 papers from the candidate pool ($\text{priority} = 0$).
      \item \textit{Stage 2 (Priority Ranker):} A continuous ranking model leveraging Multiclass Expected Gain and GBDT regression to sort worthwhile papers in descending order of utility.
    \end{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Metric Optimization via Out-Of-Fold Grid Search:} Calibrating an optimal filtering threshold $T^*$ on Out-Of-Fold cross-validation splits to maximize the competition's Harmonic Mean score between Relevance ($Q$) and Coverage ($R$).
  \item \textbf{Full Reproducibility:} Maintaining clean, modular code, documented dependencies, and exact random seeds within the official GitHub repository: \url{https://github.com/sangngng/DataMining261}.
\end{enumerate}
